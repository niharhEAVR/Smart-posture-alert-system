# Hardware Setup

```text
ESP32 GPIO25
      |
     1kΩ
      |
   Base (middle leg)
      |
   2N2222A
      |
Motor ---- 3.3V
      |
   Diode
```

More accurately:

```text
3.3V ---- Motor ---- Collector
                        |
                     2N2222A
                        |
                      Emitter
                        |
                       GND

GPIO25 ---- 1kΩ ---- Base
```

---

# Why couldn't we connect the motor directly to GPIO?

An ESP32 GPIO pin can only provide a **small amount of current**.

Think of current like water flow:

* GPIO pin = small tap
* Motor = machine that needs more water

When the motor starts spinning, it needs much more current than the GPIO can safely provide.

If connected directly:

❌ Motor may not spin properly
❌ ESP32 may reset
❌ GPIO may get damaged

---

# What does the transistor do?

The **2N2222A acts like an electronic switch**.

Think of it as:

```text
GPIO signal
      ↓
Transistor switch
      ↓
Motor gets power
```

### When GPIO25 = LOW (0V)

```text
GPIO OFF
   ↓
Transistor OFF
   ↓
Motor OFF
```

No current flows through the motor.

---

### When GPIO25 = HIGH (3.3V)

```text
GPIO ON
   ↓
Transistor ON
   ↓
Motor gets power
   ↓
Motor vibrates
```

A tiny current from the GPIO controls a much larger current through the motor.

---

# What was the 1kΩ resistor doing?

The resistor protects the ESP32 and transistor.

Without it:

```text
GPIO → Base
```

Too much current could flow into the transistor's base.

With the resistor:

```text
GPIO → 1kΩ → Base
```

The current is limited to a safe value.

Think of it as a speed breaker for electricity.

---

# What was the diode doing?

Motors are **inductive loads**.

When a motor suddenly stops, it behaves like a tiny generator and can create a voltage spike.

```text
Motor stops
      ↓
Voltage spike generated
      ↓
Could damage transistor/ESP32
```

The diode catches this spike safely.

This diode is often called a **flyback diode** or **freewheel diode**.

Think of it as a safety valve.

---

# Current Flow When Motor Runs

When GPIO25 becomes HIGH:

```text
3.3V
  ↓
Motor
  ↓
Collector
  ↓
Emitter
  ↓
GND
```

This current path powers the motor.

At the same time:

```text
GPIO25
  ↓
1kΩ resistor
  ↓
Base
```

This tiny current turns the transistor ON.

---

# Real-world Analogy

Imagine:

* ESP32 = your finger
* Transistor = light switch
* Motor = room fan

Your finger cannot power the fan directly.

But your finger can flip a switch.

```text
Finger → Switch → Fan
```

Similarly:

```text
ESP32 → Transistor → Motor
```

The ESP32 doesn't power the motor directly; it **controls the transistor**, and the transistor switches the motor's power.

### One-line viva answer

> The 2N2222A transistor is used as a switch to drive the vibration motor because the ESP32 GPIO cannot supply enough current. A 1kΩ resistor limits base current, and a flyback diode protects the circuit from voltage spikes generated by the motor.
