### For a quick portable setup

You can connect:

```text
TP4056 OUT+  ---> ESP32 VIN
TP4056 OUT-  ---> ESP32 GND
```

and

```text
Battery Red   ---> TP4056 B+
Battery Black ---> TP4056 B-
```

So the complete power path is:

```text
LiPo Battery
     ↓
   TP4056
     ↓
OUT+ ----> VIN (ESP32)

OUT- ----> GND (ESP32)
```

### Will it work?

Usually yes, especially with a freshly charged battery:

```text
Battery full = 4.2V
Battery normal = 3.7V
```

Many ESP32 DevKit boards run fine from that.

### The limitation

As the battery discharges:

```text
4.2V → 3.9V → 3.7V → 3.5V
```

the ESP32 may eventually become unstable or reset because the onboard regulator has less headroom.

### The "proper" version

For a wearable/product:

```text
Battery
   ↓
TP4056
   ↓
MT3608 Boost Converter
   ↓ 5V
ESP32 VIN
```

This keeps the ESP32 getting a stable supply even when the battery voltage drops.

### What I would do for your project

For now:

```text
Battery → TP4056 → ESP32 VIN/GND
```

Build it and test it.

If it works reliably for your motor project, you're done.

If later you notice:

* random resets
* Wi-Fi disconnects
* ESP32 shutting down when battery gets low

then add an MT3608 boost converter.

One more useful thing: add a small slide switch between `OUT+` and `VIN` so you can turn the whole wearable on and off without unplugging the battery. That makes it feel like a real portable device.
